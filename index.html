A simplified grid, not a to-scale map — provinces are arranged in roughly their real compass positions so you can orient yourself, but shapes and borders aren't literal. Limpopo carries three nations because Venda (Soutpansberg, in the far north), Pedi (Sekhukhuneland, central-east) and Tsonga (the Mozambique-border lowveld, east) occupy different parts of the same province. Many Basotho live in the independent Kingdom of Lesotho itself, which sits embedded within South Africa near the Free State/KwaZulu-Natal/Eastern Cape border but isn't a South African province and isn't drawn as its own tile here. All nine nations also have large populations in Gauteng's cities, a result of 20th-century labour migration not shown here. This tool doesn't yet cover the Khoikhoi and San peoples, South Africa's indigenous inhabitants before Bantu-speaking migration — their naming and clan traditions don't map onto the surname/praise-poem format used here, not because they matter less.

Tsonga

Southern Mozambique, Limpopo & the Eswatini border

Food

Xigugu
Dry maize kernels pounded into a dense, protein-rich powder, eaten as a snack or mixed into a fuller meal.
Tihove
A samp of dried corn cooked with peanuts, ground nuts and beans, served alone or with meat.
Vukanyi
A fermented drink made from squeezed marula fruit juice.
Matomani
Mopane worms, hand-harvested and sun-dried — both a food staple and a small-trade product.

Attire

Xibelani
A pleated, flared knee-length skirt built to swirl dramatically during the xibelani hip dance.
Nceka
A shawl and matching headwrap, usually in bold prints.
Madeha & masha
Layered beaded bracelets, anklets and necklaces.

Sources: The Grace — Tsonga traditional food · Briefly.co.za — Tsonga culture · Wikipedia — Xibelani dance

Venda

Soutpansberg & the Limpopo river valley

Food

Tshidzimba
A mix of beans, groundnuts and maize — one of the most common everyday dishes.
Thophi
Maize meal cooked with lerotse, a type of melon.
Mopane worms
Eaten cooked or dried; often compared in taste to poultry.
Baobab & milk
Milk blended with the tart flesh of baobab fruit. Pork is traditionally avoided.

Attire

Minwenda
The everyday and ceremonial wrap-style attire of Venda women, in vivid patterned cloth.
Beads & amulets
Inherited neck adornment believed to keep ancestors close to the living.
Domba aprons
Small front-and-back aprons worn by initiates in the Domba (python) dance.

Sources: Briefly.co.za — Venda culture · African Pride Collection — the story of minwenda

Pedi (Bapedi)

Sekhukhuneland & the eastern Limpopo highveld

Food

Dikgobe / bogobe
Coarsely ground corn cooked together with beans into a thick porridge.
Thophi
Maize meal with lerotse melon, shared with neighbouring Sotho-speaking groups.
Mashotja
Mopane worms, eaten as a snack or stew ingredient.
Moroga wa dikgopana
Wild greens, cooked and then sun-dried for later use.

Attire

Documentation of Bapedi-specific traditional dress was thin in the sources consulted for this tool — beadwork and leather skirts are well documented for the wider Sotho-speaking cultural family, but a confidently Pedi-specific breakdown isn't sourced here yet.

Sources: southafrica.net — Bapedi history, traditions, culture and food

Zulu

KwaZulu-Natal

Food

Umngqusho
Samp and sugar beans slow-cooked with butter, onion, potato and chilli — said to have been Nelson Mandela's favourite dish.
Isitambu
A Zulu version of pounded, stamped corn, in the same spirit as umngqusho.
Amasi
Traditionally fermented milk, eaten with pap as an everyday staple.

Attire

Isidwaba
A black cowhide or goatskin skirt for married or betrothed women, treated as an "ancestral blanket" and traditionally buried rather than discarded.
Ibheshu
A calf-skin loincloth for men — knee-length for younger men, ankle-length for elders; leopard skin was reserved for royalty and chiefs.
Umqhele
A circular fur headband historically worn by warriors and high-status men.
Beaded "love letters"
Small beaded plaques exchanged between young people, where bead colour and pattern carried coded romantic meaning.

Sources: Wikipedia — Isidwaba, Umngqusho, Umqhele · Eshowe — traditional Zulu clothing · Ulwazi Programme — love letters

Xhosa

Eastern Cape

Food

Umngqusho
Samp and beans, a dish shared with Zulu and other neighbouring cuisines.
Umphokoqo
A crumbly maize porridge, classically eaten mixed with soured milk (amasi) as umvubo.

Attire

Umbhaco
Both the durable cotton cloth and the garments made from it — dresses, wraparound skirts, long shoulder scarves and headdresses in white, ochre, orange, red, turquoise and black.
Beadwork
A nearly 200-year-old aesthetic tradition of beaded collars and accessories worn over umbhaco, for weddings, rites of passage and everyday dress.

Sources: Umbhaco.co.za · Wikipedia — Umngqusho

Ndebele

Mpumalanga, Gauteng & Limpopo

Food

Amadumbe
A tropical root vegetable, boiled, fried, or ground into meal.
Mutakura
A protein-dense mix of sugar beans, peanuts, maize, bambara nuts and cowpeas, soaked and slow-boiled.
Amacimbi
Mopane worms, collected in the rainy season and sun-dried.

This list draws partly on sources covering the wider Ndebele-speaking world, including Zimbabwe's amaNdebele — dedicated South African (Southern) Ndebele food sourcing is thinner.

Attire

Nguba
A striped blanket in green, red, blue, yellow and brown, worn over the shoulders.
Isigolwani
Thick beaded hoops worn around the neck and legs by married women, with copper and brass rings.
Isiphephetu
A beaded apron given by a mother to a daughter entering womanhood at initiation.
Iporiyana
A men's animal-skin breastplate, given by a father to his son after initiation.

Sources: Vuk'uzenzele — Ndebele traditional dress · The Grace — Ndebele traditional food

Tswana

North West

Food

Seswaa (loswao)
Beef or goat, boiled until tender then pounded, lightly salted — served at weddings, funerals and national celebrations. Called loswao in South Africa's north and west.

Beyond seswaa, dedicated Tswana-specific dish documentation was thin in the sources consulted — pap and sorghum porridge are staples shared across the wider Sotho-Tswana region.

Attire

Shweshwe & toishi
Printed cotton cloth, traditionally in shades of blue — often called the defacto Tswana cultural colour.
Mogagolwane
A checkerboard-patterned blanket worn by married women at weddings, initiations and funerals.
Makgabe
A small beaded skirt worn by young girls.

Sources: Wikipedia — Seswaa · The Grace — Tswana traditional attire

Sotho (Basotho)

Free State, plus the independent Kingdom of Lesotho

Food

Maize, millet & legumes
The historic staple diet — maize, millet, melons, pumpkins, peas, beans and groundnuts — alongside seasonal wild berries.
Jwala
Traditional sorghum beer, brewed for ceremonial occasions.

Cattle were the most valued form of wealth and central to the diet, though specific dish names beyond these staples were thin in the sources consulted.

Attire

Mokorotlo
The conical straw hat woven from moseha grass, its shape said to echo Mount Qiloane near Thaba Bosiu — now Lesotho's national emblem.
Lehlosi
A wildcat or leopard-skin blanket, historically worn only by chiefs.
Setipe & mokhahla
Other traditional skin blankets worn by men.

Sources: southafrica.net — the culture of Basotho · Wikipedia — Mokorotlo, Moshoeshoe I

Swati

Mpumalanga & the Kingdom of Eswatini

Food

Lipalishi
Maize porridge, the everyday staple.
Buganu
A fermented marula-fruit drink, central to an annual harvest festival.
Ligusha
A soup made from jute (wild spinach-like) leaves.
Imbasha
Roasted mealies with peanuts.

Attire

Emahiya (lihiya)
Bright, patterned cloth — the core of Swati dress for both men and women, worn wrapped over the torso.
Emajobo
Leather aprons worn by men over a colourful underskirt.
Ligwalagwala
Red turaco feathers, worn as a headdress reserved for royalty.

Sources: Briefly.co.za — Swazi culture, food and traditional attire

How these clans are organised

Each of these nations groups surnames into patrilineal clans, known by a different name in each language: xivongo (plural swivongo) among the Tsonga, izithakazelo (clan praises) among the Zulu, Ndebele and Swati, and iziduko among the Xhosa — where the everyday surname and the ceremonial clan name are often recorded separately (Nelson Mandela's surname was Mandela; his isiduko was Madiba). The Tswana and Sotho (Basotho) instead organise clans around a seboko (plural diboko) — a totem animal, plant or natural feature the clan is named for and identifies with, such as the crocodile (kwena), lion (tau) or baboon (tshwene). Similar clan and chieftaincy structures run through the Venda and Pedi royal houses, though this tool doesn't yet have a confidently sourced term for them in either language.
